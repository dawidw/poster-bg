Polish geometric posters, and what we built from them

Notes from the Polish School of Posters, Fangor, Zamecznik, the Cyrk circus posters and the Polish constructivists, turned into a list of motifs. Every card below is either drawn live by the generator or still a rough sketch.

What the sources say

The school

Economy of form, expressive irony and new lettering. In the 1960s and 70s artists also worked with abstraction and op art, including blurred circles and waves of color. Wikipedia PL

Zamecznik

A poster as "a mathematical operation": two or three elements, built from prefabricated parts. Geometric repetition, inversions, hand lettering, restrained palettes. Sound drawn as an oscillogram zigzag or waves spreading in circles. FAF

Fangor and Stańczak

Fangor: pulsating squares of luminous color ringed by darker, softly diffusing bands. Stańczak, who gave op art its name in 1964: narrow vibrating stripes, straight and wavy, concentric squares, grids. Fangor, Stańczak

Cyrk and Hilscher

One theme, a metaphor, geometric reduction, high-contrast color. Second-generation designers worked in a more restrained, often abstract way. Circopedia

Lenica, Młodożeniec, Świerzy

Lenica: paper cut-outs. Młodożeniec: bright stains in thick black outlines, strong lettering. Świerzy: abstract rhythmic waves of color for Warsaw Autumn. Cinema Waves, Polish Poster

Constructivism

Szczuka, Żarnower, Strzemiński and Kobro: Strzemiński's unism (unity of color, line and rhythm), typography built from letters like blocks. Wikipedia, MoMA

Motifs

Built motifs open in the generator with the same recipe. Planned ones are concept sketches.

Palettes that go with them

  • Zamecznik: black, white and one cold blue or red accent.
  • Cyrk: saturated primary pairs on cream, with black.
  • Młodożeniec: pink, yellow, green and orange on white with black line.
  • Constructivists: red, black and cream.

Caveat

These are style ideas and techniques. The motifs stay generic (stripes, rings, waves, cut-outs), never a reconstruction of a specific poster or its lettering. The descriptions come from public pages about the artists, not from studying the posters one by one.
