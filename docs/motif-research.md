# Polish geometric posters: research and what got built

Research notes behind the motifs in poster-bg, with the status of every idea. A friendlier, illustrated version lives on the [research page](https://studyofspace.gallery/research/). "Sourced" points come from the pages linked at the end; the motif list is my own reading of them.

## What the sources say

- The Polish School of Posters stands for economy of form, expressive irony and new lettering. In the 1960s and 70s artists also worked with abstraction and op art, including blurred circles and waves of color. ([Polska szkoła plakatu](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polska_szko%C5%82a_plakatu))
- **Wojciech Zamecznik** treated a poster as "a mathematical operation": two or three elements, a visual sign plus photography, built from prefabricated parts rather than brushwork. Motifs include geometric repetition (multiplying, rotating and doubling a form), inverted and magnified images, hand-drawn lettering and restrained or low-key palettes. For music posters he drew sound as a zigzag oscillogram or as waves spreading in circles. A 1961 film poster is described as vertical and horizontal black and white stripes with a large black circle and blue bars. ([Fundacja Archeologia Fotografii](https://faf.org.pl/galleries/wojciech-zamecznik/creativity/posters/?lang=en), [Cinema Waves](https://cinemawavesblog.com/film-blog/polish-school-of-posters-explained/))
- **Wojciech Fangor** (1922 to 2015) is tied to op art and color field painting. Between 1953 and 1961 he designed over a hundred posters, and with Stanisław Zamecznik he presented Study of Space in 1958, the first spatial installation in Poland. His mature work: pulsating squares of luminous color ringed by progressively darker bands that softly diffuse into the surrounding field. ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wojciech_Fangor))
- **Julian Stańczak** (1928 to 2017): "op art" was coined in 1964 in Donald Judd's review of his show. His fields of narrow vibrating stripes, straight and wavy, concentric and checkerboard squares and saturated grids. ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julian_Stanczak))
- **Cyrk (circus) posters**: one theme, a metaphor, geometric reduction, high-contrast color. Second-generation designers Hubert Hilscher, Jan Lenica, Wiktor Górka and Maciej Urbaniec worked in a more restrained, often abstract way. ([Circopedia](https://www.circopedia.org/The_Polish_%22Cyrk%22_Posters))
- **Jan Lenica**: from the 1960s the paper cut-out became a signature technique; his Wozzeck poster of 1964 is an icon of the decade. **Jan Młodożeniec**: colorful stains surrounded by thick black lines, a lot of attention to lettering, childlike stylisation. ([Cinema Waves](https://cinemawavesblog.com/film-blog/polish-school-of-posters-explained/))
- **Waldemar Świerzy** designed Warsaw Autumn posters in 1961, 1967, 1968 and 1969, among them abstract compositions of rhythmic color waves. ([Polish Poster](https://polishposter.com/3817-the-warsaw-autumn-polish-poster.html))
- **Józef Mroszczak** laid large areas of unrestricted color for a decorative effect, co-founded Projekt in 1956 and later the International Poster Biennale. **Hubert Hilscher** was a founder of the Biennale and art director of Projekt for 22 years. ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J%C3%B3zef_Mroszczak))
- **Constructivism**: Szczuka, Stażewski and Miller showed together in 1921; Miller's posters build compositions out of words and letters. Strzemiński's unism asked for unity of color, line and rhythm. ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/W%C5%82adys%C5%82aw_Strzemi%C5%84ski), [MoMA](https://www.moma.org/docs/press_archives/5347/releases/MOMA_1976_0006_6.pdf))

## Palettes that go with them

- `zamecznik`: black, white and one cold blue accent.
- `stanczak`: navy, red, cream and blue for vibrating stripes.
- `konstruktywizm`: red, black and cream.
- `lenica`: warm paper with black, red, yellow and blue.
- `jazz`: yellow to red ground with black, cream, navy and red.
- `mlodozeniec`: pink, yellow, green, orange and sky blue on white with black line.
- `cyrk`: saturated primary pairs on cream, with black.

## Ideas still open

- Typography as building blocks (Miller): letters as bars and blocks. Needs a font or hand-built glyphs, and the lettering caveat below.
- A paper-grain or halftone texture pass over any motif.
- Unism: dense fine bands where one modulation carries the whole picture.

## Caveat

These are style ideas and techniques. The motifs stay generic (stripes, rings, waves, cut-outs), never a reconstruction of a specific poster or its lettering. The descriptions come from public pages about the artists, not from studying the posters one by one.

## Women artists

Added after a second round of research, on Polish women only. Sourced: Jarema (abstract forms from music and dance, Kraków Group), Żarnower (Blok, the first Polish constructivist group, election posters and photomontage), Kobro (spatial sculptures, Blok, Praesens, Abstraction-Création), Gołkowska (reliefs of wooden blocks, Open Systems 1968), Abakanowicz (the Abakans of the 1960s), Stryjeńska (Slavic art deco). The motifs are my reading of those descriptions, not copies of any work, and the palettes `jarema`, `blok`, `kobro`, `golkowska`, `abakan` and `stryjenska` are my own choices of color. Poster artists Maria Ihnatowicz, Anna Huskowska, Wanda Telakowska and Olga Binder-Siemaszko are named in the literature but I found no description of their style, so they are not a motif yet.
