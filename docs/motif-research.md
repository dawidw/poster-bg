# Polish geometric posters: motifs worth adding

Short research note to decide which motifs to build next. "Sourced" points come from the pages linked at the end; "my reading" points are suggestions, not claims about the posters.

## What the sources say

- The Polish School of Posters stands for economy of form, expressive irony and new lettering. In the 1960s and 70s artists also worked with abstraction and op art, including blurred circles and waves of color. ([Polska szkoła plakatu](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polska_szko%C5%82a_plakatu))
- **Wojciech Zamecznik** treated a poster as "a mathematical operation": two or three elements, a visual sign plus photography, built from prefabricated parts rather than brushwork. His motifs include geometric repetition (multiplying, rotating and doubling a form), inverted and magnified images, hand-drawn lettering, and restrained or low-key palettes. For music posters he drew sound as a zigzag oscillogram or as waves spreading in circles. One 1961 film poster is described as vertical and horizontal black and white stripes with a large black circle and blue bars. ([Fundacja Archeologia Fotografii](https://faf.org.pl/galleries/wojciech-zamecznik/creativity/posters/?lang=en), [Cinema Waves](https://cinemawavesblog.com/film-blog/polish-school-of-posters-explained/))
- **Wojciech Fangor** is tied to op art and color field painting, and recognised as a key figure of Polish postwar abstract art. ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wojciech_Fangor))
- **Cyrk (circus) posters**: one theme, a metaphor, geometric reduction and high-contrast color. Second-generation designers such as Hubert Hilscher, Jan Lenica, Wiktor Górka and Maciej Urbaniec worked in a more restrained, intellectual style, often abstract. ([Circopedia](https://www.circopedia.org/The_Polish_%22Cyrk%22_Posters))
- **Jan Lenica**: from the 1960s the paper cut-out became a signature technique. **Jan Młodożeniec**: colorful stains surrounded by thick black lines, a lot of attention to lettering, childlike stylisation. ([Cinema Waves](https://cinemawavesblog.com/film-blog/polish-school-of-posters-explained/), [Polska szkoła plakatu](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polska_szko%C5%82a_plakatu))
- **Waldemar Świerzy** designed several Warsaw Autumn festival posters as abstract compositions of rhythmic color waves. ([Warsaw Autumn posters](https://polishposter.com/3817-the-warsaw-autumn-polish-poster.html))

## Candidates, in the order I would build them

1. **Oscillogram** (Zamecznik, Warsaw Autumn): a zigzag or sine amplitude trace on a dark ground, plus an option with concentric rings radiating from a point ("sound waves spreading in circles"). Cheap to draw with paths, very recognisable.
2. **Stripe field with a disc** (Zamecznik, 1961 film poster): vertical stripes on one half, horizontal on the other, a large solid circle on top. Extends `stripes` and `rings`.
3. **Multiply and rotate** (Zamecznik): one simple form repeated, rotated and doubled around a centre, flat colors. Seeded count, angle and spacing.
4. **Cut-out figure** (Lenica): a few large flat paper-like shapes with straight and slightly ragged edges, layered, two or three colors on a plain ground. Close to `blob`, but hard-edged and collaged.
5. **Black-outlined stains** (Młodożeniec): bright flat blobs with a thick black outline and a deliberate offset between ink and outline (misregistration).
6. **Rhythm bars** (Świerzy, music posters): vertical bars of varying width and height in a color sequence, like a score or equaliser. A variation on `steps`.
7. **Halftone dots and moire** (op art in general): a dot grid with size driven by a soft gradient, and two rotated line grids that interfere. The generator has no halftone yet.
8. **Misregistered second ink layer** (screen-print look): an option for any motif that draws a copy shifted a few pixels in a second ink. This already sits on the "not built yet" list.

## Suggested palettes to go with them

- Zamecznik: black, white and one cold blue or red accent.
- Cyrk: saturated primary pairs on cream, with black.
- Młodożeniec: pink, yellow, green and orange on white with black line.

## Caveat

These are style ideas. The motifs should stay generic (stripes, rings, waves, cut-outs), never a reconstruction of a specific poster or its lettering.
